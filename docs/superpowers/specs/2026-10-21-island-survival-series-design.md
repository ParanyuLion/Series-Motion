# Island survival series: design

Status: draft for the user's approval. Nothing is built yet.

## Intent

The user wants a short-video series for Instagram Reels and TikTok that hooks viewers from the title alone and keeps them guessing the whole way, so they go on to watch the next part. The series is told in Thai.

The series is a survival drama about seven people who wake up on a deserted island with no explanation. The emotional engine is frustration: the hero is right, a group member undermines him, and the others hesitate to side with him, so the viewer keeps watching to see when the group finally accepts him. The mood is bleak and hopeless.

## Goals

- 5 parts, about 60 seconds each (a few seconds over is fine). Each part ends on an unresolved question that makes the viewer want the next part.
- Vertical 9:16 video. All pictures are drawn by code (SVG, rendered with Remotion), not generated by AI video models. The existing pilot in `E:\Coding\animation\Remotion` (character rig, parallax world, Thai narration, synthesised music and sound effects) is the base to build on.
- Excitement or tension throughout every part; no flat stretches.
- Part 1 has an opening hook animation. Every part ends with a short outro that says to watch the next part. The outro is on top of the 60 seconds, not counted in it.
- Each part can include an explainer animation: a map of the island showing who is where and what they are doing, or a diagram of a character's plan.

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- No AI-generated video clips for the main picture.
- No separate voices for characters. One narrator only.
- Not a failure if the island mystery stays unsolved; it is deliberately left for a later season.
- Anti-goals: a villain who is simply evil with no human side; the group refusing the hero for so long that viewers lose patience; deaths bunched together so the cast empties too early; characters who look alike in a small phone-sized frame.

## Story (agreed with the user)

Setting: a deserted island. Seven people appear there with no explanation of how. The cause stays a mystery, with a new clue in each part.

Cast (the first two are the user's; the other five were proposed by the assistant and the user accepted them):

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| Hero | Fisherman. Knows the sea and the island. Handsome, bearded, muscular. Competent through experience, not talk. He is the narrator. |
| Villain | Rich young woman. Beautiful, spoiled, always indulged, so she has never been told she is wrong. Not a murderer: her ego and selfishness get friends killed, and she deflects the blame. Her designer dress gets dirtier and more torn each part. |
| Bodyguard | Loyal to the villain. Dies in part 4. |
| Content-creator teen | Thinks he knows best, ignores warnings. Dies in part 2. |
| Businessman (middle-aged) | Backs whoever seems to have more connections. Dies in part 3. |
| Nurse | Kind, privately believes the hero but dares not say so. Survives. |
| Frightened student | Wavers, but casts the deciding vote in part 4. Survives. |

Four survive: hero, villain, nurse, student. The ones who die are always ones who ignored the hero's warning.

The villain stays alive. Her guilt and her deflection are the lasting consequence, not a punishment scene.

Plan of the five parts (the user said it matches what they had in mind; details inside each part are the builder's to develop):

1. **Waking.** The seven wake on the island. The hero reads danger signs and warns the group to move. The villain takes charge and brushes the warning off. Ends on a change in the sky.
2. **First night.** The first person who ignored the hero dies. The villain says it was not her fault. Ends on a clue that nobody came here by chance.
3. **Scarcity.** Food and water run short. The villain makes an ego-driven wrong call and a second person dies. The group splits and the people in the middle begin to doubt her. Ends with the hero holding a secret about the island.
4. **The vote.** The group votes on a leader. People still hesitate to back the hero. A third person dies. The villain breaks down but still blames the hero. Ends with the hero warning that the next night will be the worst.
5. **The last night.** The four who remain finally follow the hero. The villain must choose between admitting her guilt and running from it. Ends on a big unresolved hook for the next season: a sign at dawn that the island is not done with them.

Escalation across parts: the group accepts the hero in visible steps, not all at once (for example, one person privately believes him by part 2, and the villain's support starts to crack by part 4).

## Look and feel (decided)

- Mood: bleak, hopeless, quiet. Tone "A" was chosen from two mockups: steel grey, heavy dull sea, overcast sky, thin fog. Colours stay desaturated. Light is rare and small, such as a campfire or a torch.
- Character designs follow the mockup lineup (`cast-and-tone.html` in the brainstorm session folder `.superpowers/brainstorm/`), simplified flat figures that stay distinct at phone size by hair, clothing colour and props (beard, glasses, cap, hair bun, dress). The mockups are rough; the real characters must move, walk, and gradually get dirtier or torn.
- Visual storytelling of decline: costumes dirty and tear, faces and posture worsen over the five parts.

## Voice and sound (decided)

- One narrator, in Thai, speaking as the hero in the first person ("ผม"). Calm, low, not shouting. Other characters' lines appear as subtitles or are reported by the narrator.
- Music and sound effects are synthesised in code (no licensing issues). Music is lonely and sparse; tension comes from cuts and sound effects, not from loud music.
- Thai voice currently comes from Gemini TTS (the voice "Charon", sped up 1.1x was the user's choice in the pilot). Whether it still fits a first-person, gloomy narrator is to be checked in a test; this is open.

## Explainer animations (decided in principle)

- Island map: shows who is where and what each is doing. This is the main device for explaining the situation fast.
- Plan diagrams: when a character makes a plan, show it as a simple diagram.
- Opening hook only on part 1. Closing outro on every part: a few seconds of "watch the next part".

## Constraints

- Windows 11, Node 22, ffmpeg installed, an RTX 3060 laptop GPU. Remotion is free for individuals and small companies (up to 3 people).
- Narration comes from Gemini TTS via a key in the project's `.env` file. The key is never shown or committed.
- Each part is about 60 seconds. Measured on the pilot (Gemini "Charon" at 1.1x, including natural pauses): about 7.5 Thai characters per second, excluding spaces. That is roughly 400 Thai characters of narration per 55 seconds, or 8 to 10 sentences like the pilot's. Scripts must stay near that budget and leave room for sound and for the map animations.

## Left to the builder

- Exact script text for each part, in Thai, in the hero's first-person voice, including the title that appears on the video for each part.
- The island's layout and the map design.
- How the opening hook and outro animations look.
- Shot-by-shot staging, camera moves and pacing inside each part.
- Whether to rebuild the pilot's boy character into the seven-person rig or write a new one, as long as the seven look distinct and can walk and gesture.
- Sound design details.

## Assistant's calls (settled after the builder check; the user may overrule any)

- **Base code:** reuse the pilot in `E:\Coding\animation\Remotion` (Remotion + SVG rig + parallax world + Thai narration + synthesised sound). It is a preference, not a requirement: if its single-character rig cannot scale to seven distinct, walking, gradually tearing characters, build a new rig in the same Remotion project.
- **Output specs:** 1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC audio, one MP4 per part. Burned-in Thai subtitles, kept inside the area not covered by Reels/TikTok interface (roughly inside the middle of the frame, away from the bottom and right edges). The outro (about 4 to 5 seconds, "watch the next part") is added on top of the roughly 60 seconds, so a part may run to about 65 seconds.
- **Dialogue of other characters:** the narrator reports most of what others say in his own words; short, important lines may also appear as subtitles. Both are allowed.
- **Death staging:** implied, not graphic. No blood and no on-screen drowning or bodies; use silhouettes, sound, cuts away, and the narrator's reaction. This fits the bleak style and the platforms' content rules.
- **Voice fallback:** if Charon does not fit a first-person gloomy narrator, try other Gemini voices first, then another provider. The voice is chosen and locked before part 1's animation is timed, because changing it later means re-timing everything.
- **Names:** the builder picks Thai names for all seven characters, including the narrator-hero, and the user approves them with the part 1 script.

## Open items (the assistant's calls, to be checked)

- First-person narration is the user's choice. The narrator's name is not decided.
- The deaths are placed in parts 2, 3 and 4. This is the assistant's proposal and the user accepted the plan, but they have not seen the detailed scripts.
- Part 5's closing hook is only described in general terms ("a sign at dawn that the island is not done with them"); the specific reveal is left to the builder to propose and the user to approve.
